# libsnd 3.7 as Breath of Fire III runs it: the reading, and the player built on it

**Status:** READ, BUILT, MEASURED (2026-10-08, a cloud session on
`audio/sequence-from-disc`, group SEQ of
[`sequenced-music-plan.md`](sequenced-music-plan.md) section 9). Every rule
below was read from `SLPS_009.90` with capstone and is cited by address; the
player in `src/audio/seq.{h,cpp}` reproduces them. Against the Mednafen
renders of all 165 songs the player's output equals the render to within one
LSB wherever the game's interrupt timing is reproduced (section 9.1: 160 of
165 at >= 0.99 of the 0.25 s windows, the five below read in 9.6), and the
loop period is the render's to the VSync on all 156 - to the sample on 112,
by one on 44, the jitter below. What is not reproduced is the game's own VBlank
interrupt latency, a jitter of about half a sample (sd) on each tick, which
costs the percussion-heavy songs up to a third of their windows (section 9.4).

Sources: the boot EXE `SLPS_009.90` from the JP disc (`tools/psx_disc.py get`,
scratch only); its Psy-Q 3.7 `LIBSND` / `LIBSPU` functions as the sibling's
`symbols.toml` names them (whole-object signature matches, "370"); a
disassembler of our own in scratch (`mdis.py`: capstone, MIPS32 LE, symbol
names, `lui`/`addiu` pairs resolved). No emulator code was read. Values
derived from the EXE are given as numbers and formulas, never as dumps; the
two small library tables the player needs are either generated by a formula
checked against the EXE (the pitch table, 3.4) or equal to the public SPU
description (the reverb preset, 6.2).

**The EXE's load address is `0x80093800`, not `0x80010000`**: the PS-X EXE
header has `t_addr = 0x80093800`, `t_size = 0x163800` (the file less its
0x800-byte header), `pc0 = 0x8014AA0C`. File offset = address - `0x80093800`
+ `0x800` (as `bgm-comparison.md` 6.2 already uses for the song table).

## 1. The tick

### 1.1 What the game calls, in order

The sound start-up is `0x8015D6F0` (called from `Boot_Init` `0x8014E974`):

| Address | Call | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `0x8015D6F8` | `SsInit()` | `_SpuInit(0)` + `_SsInit` (1.1.1) |
| `0x8015D70C` | `SsSetTableSize(0x80148A4C, 2, 4)` | 2 SEPs of 4 sub-songs; every score's "next SEP" byte `+3C` = `0xFF` (`0x8016E22C`) |
| `0x8015D714` | `SsSetTickMode(1)` | `SS_TICK60`: 1.2 |
| `0x8015D71C` | `SsUtSetReverbType(1)` | `SPU_REV_MODE_ROOM`: 6.2 |
| `0x8015D724` | `0x80170030` = `SsUtReverbOn` | `SpuSetReverb(SPU_ON)`: ATTR bit 7 |
| `0x8015D72C` | `0x8017412C(0)` | `0x8018F304 = 0`: the ENVX auto-free of `_SsVmFlush` on (3.9) |
| `0x8015D734` | `SsSetReservedVoice(16)` | the sequencer's voices are 0..15 (`0x8018EBDC = 16`) |

then in `Boot_Init`: `SsUtSetReverbDepth(40, 40)` (`0x8014E9C8`), `SsSetRVol(40,
40)` (`0x8014E9D4`), and `0x8015D860`: `SsStart2()`, `SsSetMVol(127, 127)`,
`SsSetSerialAttr(SS_SERIAL_A, SS_MIX, SS_SON)`, `SsSetSerialVol(SS_SERIAL_A,
110, 110)`.

#### 1.1.1 The registers at start

`_spu_init` (`0x80168CEC`): MVOL 0, ATTR 0; after the busy wait, every voice
VOL 0/0, PITCH `0x3FFF`, SSA `0x200`, ADSR 0/0; the 16 bytes at `0x80184468`
(all `0x07`: a self-looping block) to SPU address `0x1000` by I/O; KON all,
four `_spu_FsetDelay` waits, KOFF all; ATTR `0xC000`. `_SpuInit` then sets ESA
to mode 0's address `0xFFFE` (`0x801698E0`). `_SsInit` (`0x8016BB3C`) writes
the eight halfwords at `0x80184D88` to every voice (VOL 0/0, PITCH `0x1000`,
SSA `0x3000`, ADSR `0x00BF`/0, ENVX 0, LSAX 0) and the sixteen at `0x80184D98`
to `0x1F801D80..` (MVOL `0x3FFF`/`0x3FFF`, EVOL, KON, KOFF, PMON, NON, EON all
0), then `_SsVmInit(24)` (`0x801715FC`): every voice record free, age 24,
`seqid` -1, `vag` `0xFF`, tone `0xFF`, pan 64; damper 0; mono flag
`0x8018EBBE` 0. `SsSetMono` / `SsSetStereo` (`0x8017413C` / `0x80174150`)
are called from the option screens' overlays `START.EMI` and `STATUS.EMI`
(the player's stereo / mono setting): with the flag set, each volume
function gives both sides the louder side's level before the square law
(3.6; `MusicSynth::SetMono`).

### 1.2 `SsSetTickMode(1)` (`0x8016E344`)

`GetVideoMode()` (`0x80175D54`, reads `0x8018606C` = 0, NTSC). The argument
indexes the jump table at `0x8014A0C4` (six entries): 0 -> 60 or 50 by video
mode, **1 -> `_snd_seq_tick` (`0x80190C78`) = 60** (`0x8016E3F4`), 2 -> 240,
3 -> 120, 4 -> 50, 5 -> 60 or 50. For mode 1 on NTSC the stored tick mode
`0x80184DB8` becomes 5 (the delay slot at `0x8016E3E0` / `0x8016E408`), which
is what `_SsStart` acts on: the sequencer runs **once a VSync**, its
arithmetic using 60 per second as the nominal rate. (So `SS_TICK60` = 1,
`SS_TICKVSYNC` = 5 in this library.)

### 1.3 `SsStart2` (`0x8016CE10`) -> `_SsStart(0)` (`0x8016CB88`)

Mode 5: `0x80184DCA` = 0 and, since the argument is 0, `0x80184DC8` = 1, so
(`0x8016CD14`..) `VSyncCallback(*0x80184DC0)` with `0x80184DC0` =
`SsSeqCalledTbyT` (`0x8016CEC0`, its initial value in the EXE). No root
counter is programmed and `_SsSeqCalledTbyT_1per2` (`0x8016CE70`, every other
call) is installed only on the root-counter path: **not in use**.

### 1.4 `SsSeqCalledTbyT` (`0x8016CEC0`), once a VSync

1. A re-entrancy flag `0x8018EBC0`.
2. **`_SsVmFlush()` first** (3.9): the register writes the previous tick's
   events made reach the SPU now, at the start of the next tick.
3. For each open SEP (`0x80191500` = 2) and sub-song (`0x80191502` = 4),
   score `+90` flags: bit 0 (play) -> `Snd_play` -> `_SsSeqPlay` (1.5), then
   bit 4 `_SsSndCrescendo`, bit 5 `_SsSndDecrescendo`, bits 6/7 `_SsSndTempo`
   (accelerando / ritardando - never set by the game); outside the play bit:
   bit 1 `_SsSndPause`, bit 3 `_SsSndReplay`, bit 2 `_SsSndStop` and the flags
   cleared.

### 1.5 The arithmetic: `_SsInitSoundSep` (`0x8016BE30`), `_SsSeqPlay` (`0x8016D784`)

The score (0xAC bytes, table `0x80190C80[sep] + sub * 0xAC`) keeps time in
**tenths of a tick**: `_SsReadDeltaValue` (`0x8016B44C`) returns the
variable-length delta times 10 (`0x8016B4E4..0x8016B4F0`).

| Quantity | Rule | Address |
|---|---|---|
| bpm (`+84`, `+8C`) | `60,000,000 / tempo`, **rounded up when the remainder exceeds `tempo / 2`** (`tempo >> 1 < rem`) | `0x8016BFEC`..`0x8016C03C` |
| step (`+70`, `+72`): tenths of a tick per VSync | `x = resolution * bpm * 10`; if `x >= 60 * 60`: `x / 3600`, **+1 when the remainder exceeds `30 * 60` = 1800**; `+6E` = -1 | `0x8016C0D8`..`0x8016C1B4` |
| slow mode (`x < 3600`) | `+6E` = `+70` = `600 * 60 / (resolution * bpm)`: VSyncs per tenth | same |
| a tempo meta event | bpm = `60,000,000 / tempo` **truncated** (no rounding), step as above | `_SsGetMetaEvent` `0x8016B114` |

`_SsSeqPlay`, with `d` = `+88` (tenths to the next event) and `s` = step:

- if `d - s > 0`: in normal mode `d -= s` and return (slow mode counts `+6E`
  down instead);
- else: read events (`_SsGetSeqData`) - each event's handler reads the delta
  after it into `+88` - accumulating `acc = d + delta` and continuing while
  the new delta is 0 or `acc < s`; then `+88 = acc - s`.

So an event fires on the first VSync whose cumulative step reaches it, the
remainder carried exactly, with one asymmetry: the first event of a call is
taken at `acc <= s`, the following ones only at `acc < s` (one exactly on the
boundary waits a VSync, unless it is the first).

**The measured periods come out of it.** Song 0: resolution 48, tempo
681,818 us -> bpm 88 -> `x` = 42,240 -> 11 rem 2,640 -> step **12** (11.73
nominal); the loop is 7,104 ticks -> 71,040 / 12 = **5,920 VSyncs**. Song 153:
step 18 (`48 * 136 * 10 / 3600` = 18.13), **2,560 VSyncs**. The renders' loop
periods are 4,363,852 and 1,887,071 samples (`loops.json`): 737.13716 and
737.13711 samples a VSync. **Mednafen's NTSC frame is 263 lines of 3,412.5 GPU
clocks at its GPU/CPU ratio 103896/65536, over 768 CPU clocks a sample: 737.137137
samples** - the only simple combination inside both measurements' rounding
(263 x 3,413 gives 737.245; the true 53.693 MHz ratio 737.136). So the
0.981 / 1.010 of `bgm-comparison.md` 7.2 are the step's rounding (12 for
11.73; 18 for 18.13) times the VSync rate (59.826 Hz against the nominal
60). The player uses exactly `263 * 6825 * 65536 / (103896 * 768 * 2)` samples
a VSync, carried as a fraction (`seq.h` `kTickNum` / `kTickDen`).

No song is in slow mode (the smallest `resolution * bpm` is 2,352, song 26,
against the 360 it would take)
and **no song has a tempo event** (the census, section 4).

## 2. The event reader

### 2.1 `_SsGetSeqData` (`0x8016DA58`)

One byte at the pointer `+04`: if bit 7, a status (low nibble to `+12`, the
"current channel", high nibble to `+11`); else running status from `+11`.
Handlers (the table the game fills at `0x801627B8`, 2.4):

| Status | What is read, in order |
|---|---|
| `0x9n` | note, velocity, **then the delta** (`+88`), then `_SsNoteOn` |
| `0xBn` | `_SsSetControlChange`: controller, value, the handler reads the delta |
| `0xCn` | `_SsSetProgramChange`: program to `+2C+ch`, delta |
| `0xEn` | one byte skipped (the LSB), `_SsSetPitchBend` reads **the MSB only**, delta |
| `0xFn` | the next byte: `0x2F` -> `_SsSeqGetEof`; anything else -> `_SsGetMetaEvent`, which reads three bytes as a tempo (no length byte, no type check) and the delta |
| `0x8n`, `0xAn`, `0xDn` | **nothing is read**: the stream would desynchronise. None occurs (section 4) |

`+12` is set by every status byte and kept through running status; the
volume functions read it as "the channel" even when they act on voices of
another (3.7, 3.8).

### 2.2 The end of the track: `_SsSeqGetEof` (`0x8016D894`)

`+48` (passes) += 1. If `+46` (the loop count `SsSepPlay` was given) is 0, or
passes < `+46`: the pointer back to the first event (`+08`), `+80` = 0, `+27`
= 0, `+88` = 0 - the next event read at once. Otherwise: flags `&= ~(1|8|2)`,
`|= 0x200 | 4`, `+0C = +08`, `_SsVmSeqKeyOff` (every voice of the score keyed
off), `+88` = the step; the stop bit makes `SsSeqCalledTbyT` call
`_SsSndStop` in the same VSync. **The game always passes loop count 1**
(`0x8015DDC4`: `SsSepPlay(sep, sub, SSPLAY_PLAY, 1)`): a song without loop
markers plays once and is keyed off at its end; a looping song never reaches
its end.

### 2.3 The loop: `_SsContNrpn2` (`0x8016ABF0`) and `_SsContDataEntry` (`0x8016A430`)

- **99 = 20** (`0x8016AC64`): `+16` = 20, `+27` = 1, the delta read, then
  **`+0C` = the pointer** - the position after the marker's delta.
- **6 = n** (`0x8016A4D8`): if `+27` = 1 and `+10` = 0: loop count `+28` = n,
  `+27` = 0, `+10` = 1, delta. (A later 6 with `+27` = 0 falls through: RPN
  data if `+29` = 2, NRPN data if `+2A` = 2 - neither ever, 2.4 - else just
  the delta.)
- **99 = 30** (`0x8016AC84`): count 0 -> `+10` = 0, delta, no jump; count
  1..126 -> count - 1, delta, jump to `+0C` unless it reached 0; **count >= 127
  -> the delta read and dropped, pointer = `+0C`, `+88` = 0**.

So with the songs' 127 the loop is endless and the jump lands on the event
after the start marker **with no delay**: the time between the start marker
and its next event (the marker's delta) is not repeated. **Nothing else is
reset at the jump** - no channel volume, pan, program, pitch bend, tempo or
voice: the music runs on with whatever state the end of the body left.

**Two start markers** (songs 37 = `BGM028` sub 1, and 80 = `BGM082` sub 0):
each 99 = 20 overwrites `+0C`, so the **last one** read before the end is the
loop start. The second's data entry finds `+10` = 1 and sets nothing; `+27`
stays 1, which no later event reads. The player does the same (`Nrpn2`).

### 2.4 The controller dispatch: `_SsSetControlChange` (`0x8016AED4`)

The handler table `0x8018E4D0..` is filled by the game at `0x801627B8`
(`0x8018E4D0` `_SsNoteOn`, `D4` `_SsSetProgramChange`, `D8`
`_SsSetPitchBend`, `DC` `_SsGetMetaEvent`, `E0` `_SsSetControlChange`; the
controller entries `E8` `_SsContDataEntry`, `EC` `_SsContMainVol`, `F0`
`_SsContPanpot`, `F4` `_SsContDamper`, `F8` `_SsContNrpn1`, `FC`
`_SsContNrpn2`, `E500` `_SsContRpn1`, `E504` `_SsContRpn2`; `E4E4`, `E508..`
zero).

| Controller | Goes to |
|---|---|
| 0 | the score's VAB `+4C` = value (bank select), delta |
| 6 | `_SsContDataEntry` |
| 7 | `_SsContMainVol` |
| 10 | `_SsContPanpot` |
| **11 and 64** | **both `_SsContDamper`** (`0x8016AF7C` / `0x8016B040` read the same slot) |
| 91, 121 | slots left zero by the game: a call to address 0 |
| 98, 99, 100, 101 | `_SsContNrpn1`, `_SsContNrpn2`, `_SsContRpn1`, `_SsContRpn2` |
| anything else | the delta and nothing else |

## 3. Notes and voices

### 3.1 `_SsNoteOn` (`0x8016B2EC`)

Channel `ch` = `+12`. If the sequence volume left `+74` is 0, nothing. A
velocity > 0: unless the channel's mute bit (`+A8`) is set,
`_SsVmKeyOn(seqid, vab +4C, program +2C+ch, note, velocity, pan +17+ch)`; a
velocity of 0: `_SsVmKeyOff(seqid, vab, program, note)` (no 0x8n is ever
read). `seqid` = SEP access number | sub-song << 8.

### 3.2 `_SsVmKeyOn` (`0x801719A4`)

1. `_SsVmVSetUp(vab, prog)` (`0x80174068`): VAB < 16 and open, program < 128
   (`0x8018EBC4`, 128 for a version 7 VAB, `0x801747AC`); sets the current
   VAB's header, program and tone tables and the **program's tone block**
   `0x8018F157` from the program record's `+8` - which `SsVabOpenHeadWithMode`
   wrote as the count of programs with tones before it (`0x80174804`; the
   cache's `Program.block`).
2. The note's velocity becomes **`vel * channel volume / 127`** (the channel
   volume `+4E+2ch`, signed `/127`; `0x80171A84`). That value is what the
   voice keeps (`+08`).
3. Program `mvol`, `mpan` and tone count; block >= the header's `ps` -> no voice.
4. `_SsVmSelectToneAndVag` (`0x80173A30`): **every** tone of the program
   whose `min <= note <= max`, in tone order; each gets a voice.
5. Per tone: `_SsVmAlloc` (3.3); voice record filled (`+00` vag, `+02` age 0,
   `+08` velocity, `+0A` the channel pan, `+0C` note, `+0E` seqid, `+10`
   block, `+12` program, `+14` tone, `+16` VAB, `+18` tone priority, `+13` = 1);
   `_SsVmDoAllocate` (3.5); **if the tone's sample number is 255**,
   `vmNoiseOn` (noise) - **no music bank has such a tone** (all 2,694 tones of
   the 81 `BGM*` VABs checked) - else `note2pitch` (3.4) and `_SsVmKeyOnNow`
   (3.6).

### 3.3 `_SsVmAlloc` (`0x80172168`): which voice

Over voices 0..15 (`0x8018EBDC`):

1. The **first** voice whose `+13` is 0 **and** whose `+06` (its ENVX as the
   last flush read it, 3.9) is 0 is taken at once.
2. Otherwise, among the voices whose priority `+18` is <= the tone's: the
   lowest priority; then the lowest `+06` (unsigned); then the highest age
   `+02`. Ties keep the earlier voice.
3. None (all priorities higher): no voice, the tone is dropped.
4. Taken: every voice's age + 1, this one's 0 and its priority = the tone's.

Every music tone has priority 0 (all 2,694), so stealing goes by the lowest
envelope, then the oldest. There is no other limit than the 16 voices.

### 3.4 Pitch: `note2pitch` (`0x801723D4`), `note2pitch2` (`0x80172498`)

`note2pitch`: `x = note + 60 - centre`; `fine = shift >> 3` (at most 15);
`octave = x / 12`, `semitone = x % 12` (C division); pitch = `T[semitone * 16 +
fine]` shifted left by `octave - 5` (right if negative), 16 bits. `T` is the
192-halfword table at `0x80184DD4`: **every entry equals `floor(4096 *
2^(i/192))`** (all 192 checked; their fractional parts lie in 0.007..0.996,
so a double's `exp2` floors to them) - the player generates it. No song plays
a note more than 60 below its tone's centre (the smallest `x` is 0), so the
negative index the code would take never happens; the player aborts on it.

`note2pitch2(note, fine)` (bends): `f = (fine + shift) >> 3`; if `f >= 16`
the note + 1 and `f - 16`; then as `note2pitch`.

### 3.5 `_SsVmDoAllocate` (`0x801703F4`): address and envelope

- The voice's `+06` (ENVX) = `0x7FFF` and its bit cleared in all 16 slots of
  the zero-ENVX history (3.9).
- **SSA** = the sample's address / 8 from the program records' reserved
  halfwords (`+0C` for odd, `+0E` for even sample numbers, `(n - 1) / 2`),
  which `SsVabOpenHeadWithMode` filled with `(VAB address + the sum of the
  size table's entries 0..n-1) >> 3` (`0x80174938..0x801749A4`; entry 0 is 0,
  so sample 1 is at the VAB's address).
- **ADSR1** = the tone's, **ADSR2 = the tone's + the damper** (`0x8018EB58`:
  0, or 2 after a damper / expression >= 64; `0x801705C4`) - otherwise the
  header's words as they are.
- Dirty bits 8 (SSA) and 0x30 (ADSR).

### 3.6 `_SsVmKeyOnNow` (`0x80172B74`): the volume and the key on

With `vel` the scaled velocity (3.2), `M` the VAB header's master volume,
`pm` / `pp` the program's `mvol` / `mpan`, `tv` / `tp` the tone's `vol` /
`pan`, `cp` the channel pan at key on, `L` / `R` the sequence volume (`+74`
/ `+76`); every division truncates (the code's magic multiplies, each
checked against exact division over its range: `0x81020409` /127,
`0x82061029` /16129, `0x040C2051` /16129, `0x02040811` /127, `0x04104105` /63,
`0x82082083` /63, `0x00040011` /16383):

```
a  = vel * (M * 0x3FFF) / 16129
a  = a * pm * tv / 16129
l  = a * L / 127 ;  r = a * R / 127
tp < 64 ? r = r * tp / 63 : l = l * (127 - tp) / 63
pp < 64 ? r = r * pp / 63 : l = l * (127 - pp) / 63
cp < 64 ? r = r * cp / 63 : l = l * (127 - cp) / 63
VOLL = l * l / 16383 ;  VOLR = r * r / 16383        (fixed mode, 0..0x3FFF)
```

- **A square law** on the final amplitude; pan only ever attenuates one side.
- PITCH from 3.4; dirty bits 7 (VOL pair, PITCH).
- **Reverb: the tone's `mode` bit 2** sets the voice's EON bit, clear clears
  it (`0x80172F7C`); the mask is written whole at every flush.
- **KON |= the voice; KOFF &= ~KON** (a key on cancels a pending key off).

There is no velocity curve, no expression (controller 11 is the damper, 2.4)
and no vibrato or portamento (`vibW`/`vibT`/`porW`/`porT` are not read by
the key-on path; every music tone has them 0).

### 3.7 Key off: `_SsVmKeyOff` (`0x80171EF4`), `_SsVmKeyOffNow` (`0x80172AA4`)

**Every** voice 0..15 with this note, program, seqid and VAB is keyed off
(not only the newest). `_SsVmKeyOffNow`: `+13` = 0, `+04` = 0, `+00` = 0; KOFF
|= the voice; **KON &= ~KOFF** (a key off cancels a key on of the same tick).
It is the SPU's KOFF - the release from the ADSR - never a volume write.
`_SsVmSeqKeyOff` (`0x80173994`): every voice of the seqid.

### 3.8 Controllers on held voices

- **Volume, 7: `_SsContMainVol` (`0x8016A860`)** calls `_SsVmSetVol(seqid,
  vab, prog, value, channel pan)` **before** storing the value as the
  channel's volume. **Pan, 10: `_SsContPanpot` (`0x8016A938`)** calls it with
  the stored volume and the new pan, then stores the pan.
- **`_SsVmSetVol` (`0x80173AE4`)**: zero volume or pan becomes 1; for every
  voice of the seqid, program and VAB (any channel): if the current channel's
  stored volume is 0 and differs from the new one it becomes 1; then 3.6's
  formula with `vel` = **the voice's kept velocity (already times the old
  channel volume) times the new volume / 127**, the program of the voice, its
  tone, and the pan argument for `cp`. So a held note's level after a volume
  change is `vel * old * new / 127^2`, and a new note's `vel * new / 127`.
- **Pitch bend: `_SsVmPitchBend` (`0x80173258`) -> `_SsVmPBVoice`
  (`0x80173058`)** for every voice of the seqid, VAB and **program** - whatever
  its channel, and also while it is releasing. With `b = MSB - 64`:
  `b > 0`: `a = b * pbmax`, note += `a / 63`, `fine = 2 * (a % 63)`;
  `b < 0`: `a = b * pbmin`, `q = a / 64` (truncated), note += `q - 1`, `fine =
  2 * (a - 64q) + 127`; `b = 0`: the note, fine 0. PITCH = `note2pitch2`
  (3.4), dirty bit 4. **The bend is not remembered**: a note keyed after it
  plays unbent until the next bend event.
- **Damper / expression (64, 11)**: `0x8018EB58` = 0 below 64, 2 from 64,
  added to ADSR2 at the next key on (3.5). No song sends either.

### 3.9 `_SsVmFlush` (`0x8017101C`): what reaches the SPU, and when

At the start of every `SsSeqCalledTbyT`, in this order:

1. The zero-ENVX history: ring index `0x8018F470` = (index + 1) & 15, that
   slot cleared; for voices 0..15 `+06` = ENVX (register `+0C`), the voice's
   bit set in the slot if 0.
2. With `0x8018F304` = 0 (1.1): the AND of **slots 0..14** (the loop bound
   is 15; slot 15 never takes part): a voice in all of them gets `+13` = 0
   (free) - about 15 VSyncs of silence after its release ends.
3. KON &= ~KOFF.
4. Per voice 0..23 (the auto-volume / auto-pan hooks at `+1C` / `+28` are
   the effects' `SsUtAutoVol` / `SsUtAutoPan`): dirty bit 0 -> VOLL, VOLR;
   bit 2 -> PITCH; bit 3 -> SSA; bit 4 -> ADSR1, ADSR2; from the shadow
   registers at `0x8018F180` (16 bytes a voice); dirty cleared.
5. **KOFF (`0x1F801D8C/E`), then KON (`D88/A`), then EON (`D98/A`)**, the
   pending masks cleared.

LSAX is never written: the sample's own loop-start flag sets it.

### 3.10 The sequence volume: `_SsVmSetSeqVol` (`0x80173348`)

`SsSepSetVol` / the ramps set `+74` / `+76` (0 -> 1, above 127 -> 127) and
re-volume every voice of the seqid with 3.6's formula over **the voice's kept
velocity times the stored volume of the current channel (`+12`) / 127** - the
channel of the last event read, not the voice's. During Music_Play's
crescendo (5.1) this is the volume every note keyed in the first eight
VSyncs keeps.

### 3.11 A new VAB: `SsVabClose` (`0x80174190`) leaves the voice records

Read 2026-10-10 (the 132 bytes, capstone over `SLPS_009.90`): with `vabid <
16` and the used byte `0x8018F170[vabid]` = 1, `SpuFree(0x80191550[vabid])`,
the used byte to 0, the open count `0x80191548` - 1. Nothing else: the
voice records `_SsVmInit` set up (1.1.1) keep their `seqid`, tone block,
tone and velocity across the close and the next `SsVabOpenHeadSticky`. So on
the PSX a voice keyed from the old VAB, released or not, still belongs to
its sequence id; the next `_SsVmSetSeqVol` (3.10), `_SsVmSetVol` or
`_SsVmPBVoice` of a sequence with that id re-volumes or re-pitches it with
the tone attributes at the old index of the **new** VAB's tone table - past
the table's end when the new bank has fewer programs (whatever the header
buffer holds there). Its sample bytes in SPU RAM are the new bank's by
then, since the new body goes to the same address (6.3).

The player cannot reproduce a read past the table, and must not index past
its own (the review finding of 2026-10-10: song A from a bank of two
programs, then song B from a bank of one, aborted in `Play`'s
`SsSepSetVol(0, 0)` on tone 16 of 16). `MusicSynth::LoadBank` therefore,
when the bank's name changes, keys every sequencer voice off
(`_SsVmKeyOffNow`) and puts each record's references into the bank back to
`_SsVmInit`'s values - `seqid` -1, tone `0xFF`, velocity 0, block, program
and note 0 - keeping ENVX, age and priority, which the allocator (3.3)
reads as the SPU's state. The release tails go on over the new bank's
samples, as on the PSX; what differs is that no later volume or bend
reaches them (on the PSX one can, with the new table's attributes, or past
it). Not measured against a render: no render crosses a bank change.
`_SsVmPBVoice` now reads its tone through the checked lookup (`ToneAt`),
aborting rather than indexing past the bank.

## 5. The game's volume calls

### 5.1 `Music_Play(song, vol, frames)` (`0x80162610`)

Song `0xFF`: nothing. Else, with `seq` / `sub` from the song table
(`0x80182830`; the SEP id from `0x80148FBC[seq]`):

1. `0x8015DCF0`: `SsSepStop(sep, sub)`; `SsSepSetVol(sep, sub, 0, 0)` - which
   `_SsVmSetSeqVol` makes **1, 1**; `SsSepPlay(sep, sub, SSPLAY_PLAY, 1)`.
2. `0x8015DE8C`: unless `0x80144950` is set, `SsSepSetCrescendo(sep, sub, vol,
   frames)`; with it set the song stays at volume 1 (a music-off setting, by
   its use; not traced further).
3. `0x80143F20` = vol, `0x80145025` = song.

The title calls it with **vol 100, frames 8** (`GAME.EMI` section 1,
`0x801D0CD0`..`0x801D0CF4`).

**The crescendo** (`_SsSndSetVolData` `0x8016E764`, `_SsSndCrescendo`
`0x8016D134`): frames < vol, so the step is `-(vol / frames)` = -12 a VSync;
each VSync while playing: remaining frames - 1, the count `+40` += step; if
`L + 12 < 127` and `(frames done) * 12 < vol`, `SetSeqVol(L + 12, R + 12)`.
From 1: 13, 25, 37, 49, 61, 73, 85, **97** - and at 0 frames left the ramp
ends. **The title's songs play at sequence volume 97, not 100.** (With frames
>= vol the ramp is instead +1 every `frames / vol` VSyncs; a ramp of 0 frames
is a division by zero on the PSX - `break 7` at `0x8016E818` - and the player
aborts.)

### 5.2 The other wrappers

| Address | What | Callers (EXE + overlays, by `jal` scan) |
|---|---|---|
| `0x8015DE28` | `SsSepSetVol(sep, sub, l, r)` at once | `GAME.EMI` |
| `0x8015DEE8` | `SsSepSetDecrescendo(sep, sub, vol, frames)` | 35 sites |
| `0x801626C0` -> `0x8015DF44` | `SsSepSetDecrescendo` **then `SsSepStop` at once**: a stop (the voices released) | 29 + 25 sites |
| `0x80162710` / `0x80162764` | `SsSepPause` / `SsSepReplay` | 24 / 19 sites |
| `0x801625AC` | load the song's file if not resident (`File_LoadRequest`) | 44 sites |

`_SsSndDecrescendo` (`0x8016D414`) mirrors the crescendo downwards: with
frames < vol, `L + step` (floor 1) each VSync while the done amount stays
within vol; with frames >= vol, -1 every `frames / vol` VSyncs; the count
reaching 0 or the frames ending stops it. **Pause** (`_SsSndSetPauseMode`
`0x8016C1E0`, `_SsSndPause` `0x8016D6B4`): play bit off now, every voice
keyed off at the next VSync. **Replay** (`_SsSndSetReplayMode` `0x8016C418`,
`_SsSndReplay` `0x8016DE44`): play bit on, from where it stopped; the notes
that were sounding are not keyed again. All implemented
(`MusicSynth::Crescendo / Decrescendo / Pause / Replay / Stop / SetVolume`).

The plan's "fade linear in libsnd's volume" holds: one volume unit a step
on the square law of 3.6, so the fade is linear in volume units and
quadratic in amplitude.

## 6. The set-up

### 6.1 Main volume, reverb depth, CD

| Call | Register value |
|---|---|
| `SsSetMVol(127, 127)` (`0x8016CA38`) | MVOLL/R = `127 * 129` = `0x3FFF`, fixed mode (`SpuSetCommonAttr` `0x8016C6A4`, mask 3) |
| `SsUtSetReverbDepth(40, 40)` (`0x8016F184`) and `SsSetRVol(40, 40)` (`0x8016CA88`) | EVOLL/R = `40 * 0x7FFF / 127` = **10,320** |
| `SsSetSerialAttr(0, 0, 1)` / `SsSetSerialVol(0, 110, 110)` | CD audio into the mix (ATTR bit 0), CD volume `110 * 258` = 28,380 - no CD audio plays under the sequenced music; the player leaves the bit clear (the SPU model takes no CD input, and its silence is what the bit would add) |

`SsSetMVol` is also called from `SCENA00.EMI` (`0x801F9C30` 0, `0x801F9C98`

`spu().SetMainVolume*` with `v * 129`.

### 6.2 The reverb

`SsUtSetReverbType(1)` -> `SpuSetReverbModeParam` (`0x8016F21C`): the mode's
68-byte record at `0x80184954 + 68 * mode` (a mask word and the 32
registers `0x1F801DC0..DFE`), its work-area start at `0x80184904 + 4 * mode`;
**EVOL is zeroed** (`0x8016F63C`), the 32 registers written
(`_spu_setReverbAttr` `0x8016F820`), ESA (`_spu_FsetRXX(0xD1, ...)`), ATTR bit 7
restored. **Mode 1's 32 values equal psx-spx's "Room" preset register for
register, and its ESA `0xFB28` is `(0x80000 - 0x26C0) / 8`**: the SPU model's
`kSpuReverbPresets[0]` is libspu's `SPU_REV_MODE_ROOM`. (For the other modes:
2..6 equal Studio Small / Medium / Large, Hall, Space Echo; 7 and 8 Chaos
Echo and Delay; 9 "Half Echo"; 0, off, is all zero with ESA `0xFFFE` - not
the spec's "Off" set.)

**Reverb never changes**: `SsUtSetReverbType`, `SsUtSetReverbDepth`,
`SsSetRVol`, `SpuSetReverb*` and `SpuSetReverbModeParam` are called only from
the boot EXE (the start-up of 1.1 and the shut-down `0x8015D8AC`: type 0,
depth 0) - a scan of all 883 non-movie files on the disc for `jal` to each.
So every scene has the title's reverb: Room, depth 40 (`plan` section 6's
per-scene question: answered, no).

### 6.3 Where the banks go

The game opens VABs with `SsVabOpenHeadSticky` (`0x801745C8`: explicit SPU
address, no `SpuMalloc`) from the slot table `0x80146778` that
`SoundSet_Layout` (`0x801621F8`) fills from `0x80182CC0 + 28 * layout`.
**The music VAB is slot 0 at SPU `0x1010` in all three layouts**; the
effects' banks: layout 0 (the title) `0x3E0A0`, `0x4F210`; layout 1
`0x3E0A0`, `0x520F0`, `0x5B170`, `0x62A90`, `0x6A3A0`, `0x71CC0`; layout 2
`0x6C6D0`. So a music bank has `0x3D090` bytes in layouts 0 and 1 and
`0x6B6C0` in layout 2 (the opening and ending banks `BGMOPN`, `BGMEND` are
`0x665F0` and `0x68B50`: layout 2 is theirs); the Room work area is
`0x7D940..0x7FFFF`; nothing overlaps. Addresses do not change the sound
(the player puts the bank at `0x1010` too). `_spu_init`'s looping block sits
at `0x1000`, just below.

## 7. Everything else the player must do (f)

- `SsSepPlay`'s loop count: 1 (2.2). `SsSetLoop` is never called.
- No accelerando / ritardando, no `1per2`, no tempo events; mono only by
  the player's option (1.1.1).
- The VSync count since boot sets the phase of the zero-ENVX ring (3.9) and
  of the VSync against the SPU's sample clock; both are unknown at a song's
  start and neither changes what is heard except through voice allocation
  ties (none observed).
- Up to 16 voices; past that the lowest envelope is stolen (3.3).
- Effects (`SE_Play`) use voices 16..23 and never touch the sequencer's.

### 9.3 Things that were not the cause

- The "noise voices" of `bgm-comparison.md` 11.1 are **not** SPU noise: no
  tone of any music bank selects noise (3.2). They are noisy samples - hi-hats,
  cymbals - for which a one-sample shift of the key on makes the waveform
  uncorrelated (song 11's hi-hat at 1.62 s: NCC 0.40 at the grid's timing,
  1.000 one sample over). A Mednafen run differs from the next by exactly that.
- The zero-ENVX ring's phase (all 16 tried on song 153's worst stretch:
  identical).

### 9.4 What is not reproduced: the game's interrupt latency

The renders' key ons sit off a uniform VSync grid by a per-VSync delay: the
oracle moves 10..60 % of the key-on VSyncs by a sample or two (sd 0.30..0.54
samples), and that alone takes every song to 0.996..1.000. The
delay correlates with the VSync's parity (the game's main loop runs at 30
fps) but not cleanly: fits of an alternating offset gave 0.4..1.1 samples
depending on the song, and within a song the pattern drifts. It is when the
VBlank interrupt is taken and how long the handler runs before
`SsSeqCalledTbyT` writes KON - the CPU's state, not the sound driver's.
A player cannot reproduce it without emulating the game's frame; the PC
engine will have its own (a callback's timing is not a VSync either). It is
heard as nothing (a sample is 23 us); it is measured as up to a third of the
windows of a percussion-heavy song (11: 0.63) below 0.99. **Recommendation:
accept the plan's 0.99 per clean window with "clean" meaning "the oracle
timing" for the comparison**, as `synth_check.py --oracle` reports it.

oracle moved them). Worth a per-voice stem of 146 against the render at
