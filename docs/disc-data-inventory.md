# The disc-data inventory: every `base/` chunk the PC holds differently from a disc

**Status:** IN PROGRESS (2026-10-13, measured; Q2 and Q4 answered and built in section 9 - the base policy `recipes/prefer.toml` with the 27A loop its first member, DIV-0114 corrected; Q1, Q3, Q5, Q6 wait on the owner)

HANDOFF item 3, the owner's words of 2026-10-12: "let's put this as a next up
action - I agree an inventory pass is what's needed". The owner prefers the
PlayStation's data wherever both the PC install and a disc carry a chunk
(prompted by the PC title's (R), DIV-0114). Today a PC + disc cache takes the
PC's chunk wherever the PC has one (`importer.get_chunk`: the player's source
order decides, and the PC is listed first); only the music follows the disc
(the 2026-10-08 call, [`unified-data-plan.md`](unified-data-plan.md) 6).

This pass changes no output. It lists every `base/` chunk of
`analysis/cache/pc-plus-us` (the PC install, the US disc and both PSP images)
whose PC form differs from a disc's, classes each, and proposes a rule. The
raw table is generated by a new tool, so the pass can be re-run:
`tools/cache_inventory.py` (section 7).

Hard rule 1: nothing below is a byte of the game. Sizes, offsets, counts,
hashes, and descriptions of what images show are structure; the renders made
to read them are in the session's scratch, not in the tree.

## 0. The answer in five lines

1. **The PC's `base/` is the Japanese PlayStation release's, byte for byte,
   except 35 chunks the port changed** (section 2): the world map's dial page
   (16), the item-type icon page (1), the edited arenas (11 + `START`), the 27A
   arenas' held loop (2) and one byte of Ryu's form data (4). Against the JP
   disc no other `base/` chunk differs in content. The 200 enemy tables, 51
   arenas and 901 sound banks differ in format only (section 3).
2. **The (R) on the PC's title is not the port's.** It is the Japanese
   PlayStation's mark, which the port kept. The US and European discs draw a ™
   in its place (section 4.1, rendered and measured). DIV-0114's "Original
   behaviour" needs correcting (section 8.2).
3. **So there is no port cosmetic change in `base/` that is known to be one.**
   Class (c) is empty of proven rows. Its candidates are the 18 port edits
   whose purpose no one has read (the arenas, the held loop, the Ryu byte).
   They are the owner's question (section 6).
4. **What a Western disc changes against the PC is regional, not the port's:**
   the title's copyright notice and mark, the dock's button legend, the
   world-map plates, `AREA004`'s walls, and a few bytes of effect and script
   data in `.data` (section 4). The PC follows Japan in all of them.
5. **Three defects of the disc-built image turned up on the way** (section 5):
   the Western title's ™ is drawn zero wide, and two map segments overreach
   (one byte of the Meteor Strike chip's lift on every build, and three bytes
   of a `twin` row on every PSX build).

## 1. Method and sources

The sources are the PC's `DAT/` and `BOF3.exe`, and all seven held discs
(JP, US, EU-English, FR, DE PSX; PSP-JP, PSP-EU). Each was verified by
`importer.identify` against `fixtures.toml`, as for a build.

`recipes/pc-zh.toml` (generated 2026-10-12) names, for each of the 3,582
chunks, every held build that carries it byte for byte and by which transform.
It also names each PSX disc's own section where a disc lacks the chunk (`own`,
`importer-transforms.md` 5). For every chunk the recipe puts in `base/` (3,307)
and every disc, the tool takes the disc's own form of the chunk. It compares
that form, untransformed, with the PC's chunk (section 7 has the forms).

What the cache took: `analysis/cache/pc-plus-us/manifest.toml` lists **all
3,307 `base/` chunks as `pc-zh:chunk`**. It was built 2026-10-10 from recipe
`480d11b9`, and every PC chunk still hashes to the current recipe's
`42c10b75`. The tool aborts on a mismatch, and none occurred.

## 4. Regional: the PC follows Japan, a Western disc differs

These rows are not the port's changes. The PC holds the Japanese release's
bytes, the JP disc agrees, and the US / European / French / German discs carry
their region's own. "The PlayStation's" does not pick one, so each row is a
question of which region.

### 4.1 The title: the copyright notice and the mark (the (R))

The title is three parts that must move together:

| Part | Where | PC | JP disc | US disc | EU-English | FR / DE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| copyright notice | `DEMO.DAT` kind 1 `0x1A080400` (VRAM 832, 256), `base/` | = JP | 2 lines | **own**: 5,062 bytes in 9 tiles | = JP | own (FR / DE: the recipe's `language` rows) |
| the mark | `FIRST.DAT` kind 1 `0x1E000200` (VRAM 960, 0), the system glyph page; `loc/zh-CN`, not `base/` | the mark's tile = JP's | **®** | **™** | ™ (its page equals the US's) | not rendered |
| `Title_Sprites` `0x653850`, 17 entries of (u, v, w, h, clut) | `.data`, `base/exe/` | = JP, all 170 bytes | | entry 1's width 12; entry 8's width 160; entry 9's v 48 | entry 1's width 12 | FR: entry 1's width 12, entry 7's width 240 |

The measurements (scratch `page.py`, `mark.py`, `markpage.py`,
`titlesprites.py`, `titleraw.py`; the renders are scratch PNGs):

- **`DEMO`'s page is the copyright notice, not the mark.** Rendered from the
  PC, JP, US and EU-English discs as 4 bpp tiles (4 tiles across): the PC,
  JP and EU-English draw two lines (the Capcom notice and "all rights
  reserved"). The US draws three, with a Capcom U.S.A. line added. The
  recipe's own rows agree: the PC's chunk is `on = [psx-jp:copy,
  psx-eu-en:copy]`, and US, FR and DE stand in their own. `FIRST.DAT`'s page
  at the same tag is not the title's: it equals every disc's.
- **The mark is `Title_Sprites` entry 1, drawn by `Title_DrawSetA` at screen
  (262, 130)** (`src/game/title_states.cpp`: `g.sprite(0x106, 0x82, 1, 1,
  ...)` after a draw mode of tpage `0x2F`, which is VRAM (960, 0) at 4 bpp).
  That is the harness's `pc-title-mark` box `[260, 128, 272, 140]`
  (`recipes/abc-known.toml`). Its texels, at (u 96, v 60) of page
  `0x1E000200`, were rendered from each source:
  - **PC: ®. JP disc: ®. PSP-JP: ®. US: ™. EU-English: ™** (the US and
    EU-English pages are byte-identical).
  - The PC's page differs from JP's in tiles 4..15 (the port's Chinese
    changes). The mark's tile (2) is JP's.
- **`Title_Sprites` follows the same split**, read from each disc's
  `GAME.EMI#1` at the table's address (`titleraw.py`):
  - JP and PSP-JP equal the PC's in all 170 bytes, entry 1 10 wide (®).
  - US, EU-English, FR and DE have entry 1 12 wide (™).
  - US alone has entry 8 160 wide and entry 9 at v 48: the third line of its
    notice.
  - FR has entry 7 240 wide.
- **So the PC's title is the Japanese PlayStation's title**, with the port's
  Chinese elsewhere on the glyph page. The owner's "no (R) on the original
  PSX" is true of the US and European releases, which draw ™.
- **The parts are coupled.** The US notice page needs the US entries 8 and 9,
  or the third line is never drawn and the second is cut at 144 pixels. The ™
  glyph needs entry 1's width 12, or its last two columns are cut. Taking one
  part without the others draws a broken title.

Class: **regional**, with a judgement (Q4). Proposed rule, if the owner wants
the Western title: **the title follows the language layer's disc**. A cache
whose language layer is built from a Western disc takes three things from
that disc:
- its notice page (`base/`: the existing `own` stand-in, why `language`);
- its mark (the glyph page's tiles 0..3, the part the port kept as Japan's);
- `Title_Sprites` entries 1, 7, 8 and 9.

A PC install under its own Chinese, or a cache with the JP disc, keeps the
® title as both releases have it. Section 9 sketches the importer change.
Under the English overlay today, the PC shows Japan's title: `loc/en-US`
carries no `0x1E000200` and no `DEMO` chunk (scratch `loclist.py` over the
cache's `loc/`).

### 4.2 The dock's button legend (`AREA128.DAT` kind 1 `0x0A081000`)

417 bytes in 2 tiles. Rendered (scratch `a128.py`), it is a three-line
button legend:

- **PC and JP:** □ confirm / × quit / △ view the memo, in Japanese.
  *Corrected 2026-10-13* ([`key-icons-by-mapping.md`](key-icons-by-mapping.md)
  9): the first icon is circle, not square - ○ / × / △.
- **US:** × OK / △ End / □ Memo.

The US legend names **different buttons for the actions**. If it is truthful,
the US build remapped them, in code this pass did not compare. The PC's code
is Japan's. Taking the US page into a PC install would then label the
buttons wrongly.

Area 128 is the dock (the sibling's `names/places.toml`, id 128).

Class: **regional, tied to code**. Proposed rule: **keep the PC's page.** The
real gap is that a PC install under English shows Japanese here, because no
language layer carries the page (`importer-transforms.md` 5). That is a
localisation item: an English legend for Japan's mapping, in the language
layer. Question Q5 is whether to read the dock's input code on the US disc
first.

### 4.6 A port defect that the derived banks reproduce

The banks are byte-identical because the importer reproduces the port's
converter, wrap and all. [`sound-import.md`](sound-import.md) 6 item 2: in 9
sample values of 5 sounds (76 containers' banks) the PC wraps where the SPU
clamps, so the PC plays a full-scale click and the PlayStation does not.

Under the owner's stance this is the reverse of a repair: **the PlayStation's
behaviour is the disc's, and the PC's is a port defect.** Proposed rule:
clamp in `vag.decode_port` when the banks are derived. That is the owner's
open call 2 there; the stance answers it. A DIV, and those 76 banks would no
longer equal the PC's. Not built.

## 5. Defects of the disc-built image found on the way

These are described for the coordinator to number. None was fixed here: this
pass changes no output.

1. **The Western title draws its mark zero wide.** `Title_Sprites` entry 1's
   width (`0x65385E`, low byte) is unplaced in the US, EU-English, FR and DE
   images (`titlesprites.py`: `placed PPPP.PPPPP`). It is recorded as a gap
   (`recipes/exe-gaps.tsv`: `game-value Title_Sprites+0xE`), but its
   consequence was not noticed. The disc holds 12 there (`titleraw.py`) and the
   PC 10, so no segment matches. The image keeps 0, and a Western disc-only
   install draws no mark.
   - The A/B/C harness's `pc-title-mark` difference (PC against PSX-EU) is
     this gap. It is not "a mark the PlayStation lacks": the real European
     disc draws ™ there.
   - Fix: a `twin` row reading the width from the disc's own `GAME.EMI#1`, the
     way the D260..D263 rows were made.
2. **The Meteor Strike chip's lifts are zero in every disc-built image.**
   `MeteorStrikeChip_Lifts` `0x65C374` is four dwords, which
   `MeteorStrikeChip_Launch` / `_Rise` read for a chip's lift.
   - Bytes `+0..+0xA` are a recorded gap on all seven builds
     (`exe-gaps.tsv`, `game-value`), so the image holds 0.
   - Bytes `+0xB..+0xF` are placed by the `BATTLE.EMI#15` segment, which
     starts at `0x65C37F`, inside the table. At `+0xE` it places 0 where the
     PC has `0x50` (run E1, `exebytes.py`).
   - So all four lifts are 0 instead of the PC's four non-zero values. The
     overreaching byte is the one E1 lists, on all seven builds.
   - The table is on the disc whole: the JP disc's `MAGIC226.EMI#3` holds the
     PC's 16 bytes at `0x801F0C68` (`seg.py`). Two `psxmap` rows already take
     the PSPs' `0x65C364` and `0x65C368` from that section
     (`recipes/exe-psp.tsv`).
   - Fix: a `twin` row from there for every build, with the segment's start
     trimmed. A `twin` row fills only unowned bytes (`psp_exe.fill`).
3. **A map segment defeats a `twin` row: `0x61FBD0..0x61FBD2`.**
   `recipes/exe-psp.tsv` already has a `twin` row for `0x61FBD0..0x61FCA8`
   from `AREA112.EMI#13` (`0x801F455C`) on psx-jp, psx-us and psx-eu-en.
   `exe-layout.tsv` makes it data that `0x61FF50` points at. But the
   `AREA111.EMI#13` segment (`0x61FB5C..0x61FBD4`) runs 4 bytes past
   `Area111_GridStart` and owns them first, so the image holds `AREA111`'s
   zeros where the PC (and `AREA112`'s section) hold three non-zero bytes. All
   five PSX builds. Fix: trim the segment, or let a `twin` row override a
   segment's overreach.

The pattern behind 2 and 3 is a segment whose end or start lands inside a
neighbour's data. E1's list of bytes that differ from the PC on every disc
contains exactly these two cases. That makes the list a cheap check for the
class: a byte every disc places differently from the PC is either a port
change or a misplacement.

## 6. The owner's questions

- **Q1. The 11 hand-edited arenas (P3, P4).** The edits nudge single sprite
  parts by a pixel-sized step, add parts, and drop never-stepped frames, in
  member 2's field animations and member 1's battle animation. Nobody has
  rendered them, so whether they repair a visible seam (perhaps one the PC's
  renderer showed and the PlayStation's did not) or are cosmetic is unknown.
  Under the stance the disc's would be the default unless they are repairs.
  Three choices:
  - (i) render both before deciding (recommended: an hour with the arena
    reader of `arena-edits.md` 1);
  - (ii) take the disc's now, so a PC + disc cache matches a disc-only one
    (DIV-0096's form);
  - (iii) keep the PC's.
- **Q2. The 27A held loop (P6).** On the PlayStation the two copies of the
  loop play at the same speed; the port slowed the second to half. Take the
  disc's (both at 4 ticks), or keep the port's?

## 7. The tool: `tools/cache_inventory.py`

```
python tools/cache_inventory.py dat --source DAT --source DISC [--source DISC ...]
                                    [--disc BUILD ...] [--cache CACHE] [--banks] [--all] --out TSV
python tools/cache_inventory.py exe --exe BOF3.exe --source DISC [--source DISC ...] --out TSV
```

`dat` walks every chunk `recipes/pc-zh.toml` puts in `base/`. For each disc
it finds the disc's own form of the chunk, using the recipe's sources and
stand-ins (`importer.sources_of`, `owns_of`) and, for a PSP,
`recipes/psp-standins.toml`. The forms:
- `same`: the section byte for byte;
- `same-type1`: decoded;
- `rule:icons` / `rule:ryud` / `rule:hold`: the raw section, compared before
  the rule;
- `format-widen`;
- `own:<why>`;
- `psp:<why>`;
- `derived-vag` (with `--banks`) or `bank`;
- `none`.

It writes one row per chunk and disc whose form is not the PC's bytes. Each
row has the sizes, the bytes that differ with their first and last offset,
the tiles for an image, and, with `--cache`, what that cache took. The PC's
chunk is hashed against the recipe first.

`exe` builds each disc's `base/exe/` image exactly as the importer writes it
(`exe_tables.disc_built`). It lists every placed byte that differs from
`BOF3.exe`'s `.data`, in runs by symbol and by how the byte was placed.

The output is derived from the player's files, so the tool refuses an
`--out` inside the repository other than `analysis/`. The classification is
this document's; the tool only measures.

### 9.2 The mechanism: `recipes/prefer.toml`

Section 9.4's draft proposed a generated table. What was built is a
**hand-kept** table. Each row is an owner's decision on one chunk, and a
generator cannot make those, so nothing would be gained by generating it.
`cache_inventory.py` stays the measuring tool.

- **A row** names a chunk of `recipes/pc-zh.toml` by `(file, kind, tag)`,
  with:
  - `from`: the disc builds that give the chunk, in preference order;
  - `emi` and `how`: the section and the transform
    (`importer.TRANSFORMS`);
  - `sha256`: the hash of the disc's form;
  - `why`: the reason.

  Names, indices and hashes only.
- **`importer.get_chunk`** first asks `preferred`. If the chunk has a row
  and a build in `from` is among the sources, the importer:
  - takes that disc's section through `how`, at any place in the player's
    order;
  - hashes it against the row and refuses a mismatch;
  - returns it as `("prefer", EMI, section)`.

  Otherwise `get_chunk` behaves as before.
- **The manifest** records the row as `<build>:prefer:<EMI#section>` with
  the body's hash, and `[meta] prefer_sha256` as the table's hash. The
  engine's cache walk accepts any `how` (DIV-0089, `dat_cache.cpp`
  `Parse`), so no `src/` change was needed.
- **`verify`** checks each `prefer` row against the table's hash and its
  `from`. It counts the container with the stand-ins (not "composed, it is
  not the PC's"), and prints the policy's line.
- **`check`** (no game data) checks each row's chunk, layer (`base/`, not
  an enemy table), builds (PSX builds `fixtures.toml` holds), transform,
  `emi` (the recipe chunk's own) and hash shape (64 hex, not the PC's own).
- **The rule says which disc.** `from = ["psx-jp"]` on both rows. So:
  - a PC + US cache still takes the PC's chunk;
  - a PC + US + JP cache takes Japan's, whatever the order;
  - a JP disc-only install takes the disc's form, where the `hold` rule
    used to give it the port's;
  - a US disc-only install still gets the port's bytes through `hold`.

**The US disc carries the same bytes.** The type-1 decode of
`BPLCHAR/BPLD27A.EMI#7` and `BPLU27A.EMI#7` is byte-identical on all five
PSX discs (JP, US, EU-English, FR, DE). Each differs from the PC's chunk in

### 9.4 The draft as written before the answers

The text below is the proposal as it stood when the questions were open. 9.2
is what was built from it.

Today `get_chunk` takes, in the player's source order, the first source the
recipe lists as an exact carrier. The disc's own section (`own`) is a
stand-in only when no listed source carries the chunk, and a rule (`icons`,
`ryud`, `hold`) is an exact carrier because its output is the PC's chunk.

The proposal: **a generated `recipes/prefer.toml`, keyed on (file, slot)**,
written by `cache_inventory.py` from this document's classification (a
`--classify` table carried in the tool, so the doc and the rows cannot drift).
Each row has a `prefer` value:

- `disc-region`: 4.1's `DEMO` page. When a PSX disc whose own section the
  recipe lists is among the sources, and the language layer being built is
  that disc's, its `own` section goes ahead of the PC's chunk.
- `disc` (only if Q1 to Q3 say so): P3 / P4 / P6 / P7. The disc's own form
  goes ahead of the PC's chunk whenever a PSX disc is a source. For P6 and P7
  that means the raw section, without the rule, which needs the rule's
  stand-in recorded in the recipe as an `own` of its own.

`get_chunk` reads the table after its exact-source loop has picked a source.
If the chunk is listed and the condition holds, it takes the listed form
instead. The manifest records `prefer` as the how, so `verify` and the
harness can tell a preferred chunk from a stand-in.

The coupled parts outside `base/dat`:

- **The glyph page's mark tiles (4.1)** belong to the Western language
  layer. `loc_build.py` would write `FIRST.DAT` `0x1E000200` as the PC's page
  with tiles 0..3 from the disc. Tiles 0..3 are the part the port left as
  Japan's; the measured difference, PC against JP, is tiles 4..15. The
  disc's tiles come from the player's disc at build time.
- **`Title_Sprites` entries 1, 7, 8, 9 (4.1)** belong to the same layer, as
  a small kind-N chunk naming the table's PC address. The DLL lays it over
  the mapped `.data` at load, as the label chunks of DIV-0064 do. The values
  are read from the player's disc at the twin place (`GAME.EMI#1`, located by
  `exe_maps`), so no Capcom table enters our code.

`importer.py check` would gain the `prefer.toml` shape check. The
`pc-plus-us` cache would change in one `base/` container (`DEMO.DAT`) and
its `loc/en-US`. Nothing else in a PC + disc cache changes unless Q1 to Q3
are answered "disc".
