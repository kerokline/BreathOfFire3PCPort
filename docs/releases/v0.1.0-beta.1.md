**Breath of Fire III - bof3x v0.1.0-beta.1** - a player release, the first
**beta**. Beta means this: two people have played the game to the end by
hand on two kinds of install, every scripted route hashes against its
reference, the crashes found in play since alpha.5 are fixed, and the one
crash report still open is named below.

bof3x is a renovation of the 2001 Chinese PC port of *Breath of Fire III*:
every function of the game's own code is our C++, our `bof3x.exe` runs it
with no code of Capcom's mapped, and the game can be installed from the PC
port, from a PlayStation disc alone, or from a PSP disc alone. **This
download holds no game data.** You add your own copy, and everything the game
reads is built on your machine from it.

## Getting started

1. Unzip into a folder with a **short path**, e.g. `C:\Games\bof3x`.
2. Run `bof3x-launcher.exe`. The first time, it opens on the **Sources**
   page; later, it is under **Configure**, the **Data** tab.
3. On the **Sources** page, add what you own:
   - the PC port's game folder (the one holding `BOF3.exe` and `DAT`), the
     2001-04-18 Chinese release, unmodified;
   - a PlayStation disc as `.cue`/`.bin` - US, Japanese, European (English),
     French or German;
   - a PSP disc as `.iso` - Japanese or European;
   - or a `.zip` holding any of these;
   - or a whole folder of them: **Folder...** checks every image in it (and
     the folders below it) and adds each one that matches a known release.

   Each one is checked against the known releases by its files' hashes, and
   told apart in one line if it is not one. The first source makes the
   install; each later one adds what it carries (its language, the PSP's
   extras), and the install is rebuilt. Expect a few minutes per setup.
4. Play.

Everything is written inside the folder you unzipped: `sources.toml`,
`mods.toml`, `cache\`, `mods\` and `game\`. Your own files are only read.

**Upgrading from an earlier alpha:** unzip over the old folder and start the
launcher. It checks your install against the release and, when a piece of it
would be built differently, names the pieces and offers to rebuild them
(**No** starts nothing and leaves the install as it is). **A disc-built
install needs that rebuild this time:** the image built from your disc now
carries an answer for every byte the disc does not hold, and the game refuses
an image built by an older release until it is rebuilt. The rebuild reuses
what it can.

**Your saves.** The game's own save files are kept through every upgrade and
load as before, and they will through every beta. **A quicksave belongs to
the build that made it:** a newer build refuses to restore it and says so on
screen (the file is not touched). The 32-bit and the 64-bit build refuse each
other's quicksaves the same way. Before you upgrade, make a game save.

## New since v0.1.0-alpha.5

This is the first public release since alpha.5. It carries alpha.6's list
(alpha.6 was tagged on 2026-10-29 and never published) and everything after it.

- **Presets.** A new **Preset** page under **Settings** chooses how much of
  the project's repair you play with: **Recommended** (the default, what you
  had before), **As shipped** (the PC port's own behaviour where our code can
  still give it), **Repaired** (only the fixes to things that were broken) and
  **PlayStation** (what the PlayStation did where the PC port differed). The
  page lists what the chosen preset turns off for your language and install.
  A preset never turns a fix on; it only turns changes off. For a finer
  hand, `off=` in `bof3x.ini` names single changes by the name the project's
  ledger gives them.
- **Crashes fixed:**
  - the Gaist's turn-to-ash effect, which overran a buffer and crashed the
    fight (issue #163);
  - Giant Growth cast first in a session, at the hit pop-up;
  - Paralyzer, cast by an enemy with no sound cue table;
  - area 135, loading a save made in some of its zones;
  - the talk button pressed at the faerie village's plaque, where the port
    read address 3 (issue #177);
  - a faerie's Chinese name read past its slot on the village's boards,
    which ended the game when the next slot held a name;
  - F5 opened in a battle drew a corrupt menu, and a load from it stopped
    the game; the menu now draws its own lines in every scene;
  - Rei's and Garr's scatter into pixels in Myria's chamber overwrote the
    sprite state past the original's room; every pixel now has room
    (`BOF3X_OFF=PixelRoom` for the original's pools).
- **Movies and sound:**
  - the two opening movies are decoded by the game itself, not by Windows'
    media player: the picture is bit-exact against ffmpeg's decode of the
    same files, and the 64-bit build shows a picture at all (Windows has no
    64-bit Cinepak decompressor);
  - the sound plays through SDL3 audio in place of DirectSound, on both
    builds; `off=SoundSdl` in `bof3x.ini` gives DirectSound back on the
    32-bit build;
  - a quick-load during a battle voice no longer plays the voice before
    the song comes back;
  - the porting house's logo movie no longer plays, so every install starts
    with Capcom's logo alone. No file is deleted; `off=CapcomMovieOnly` in
    `bof3x.ini` plays a `logos.avi` that is in the game folder.
- **Sound and music, as the PlayStation plays them** (heard through two
  full plays):
  - a sound effect that belongs to a sprite pans with its place on the
    screen, in the field and in battle;
  - the inn's night stops the town's song where it is and brings it back
    where it stopped; the PC port ran it into the jingle and lost it;
  - where the PlayStation stops a song it now cuts, where the PC port faded
    it over ten frames (the end of a fight, the title, chapter changes).
    `BOF3X_ORIGINAL=StopCuts` gives the fade back;
  - the area's song comes back after a jingle (issue #120);
  - a battle action's effect stops the sound-effect loops the last one left
    sounding;
  - the field's level cues play at their level, and the distance-gated ones
    only near their object.
- **World map and field:**
  - **Mt. Zublo and Mt. Levett are passable on the world map** once the way
    through is open: Zublo's drawn walkway from chapter 9, Levett once the
    party has crossed it door to door. A child-era save keeps Zublo's wall.
    `off=MountainPasses` for the original;
  - the **Items screen's NormalItem and CombatItem sorts** order each half by
    item id (the original only split the list in two and left each half in
    pick-up order). `off=ItemSortById` for the original;
  - the **dock's parts-menu legend** shows the words your disc painted
    (`OK` / `End` / `Memo`, French `Fin`, German `Ende` / `Notiz`) where the
    PC showed the Japanese page, and the parts list starts its lines where
    the image's release puts them (issue #178);
  - **Horis's desert directions say east** in every Western language, as
    Capcom's Japanese does; the US, European, French and German discs each
    got some of those words wrong. `off=DesertDirections` for the disc's
    text;
  - the Desert of Death draws whole at every width (issue #185);
  - a line under the ship's ENGINE gauge and the border of the crane button
    sampled a texel past the edge; menu and HUD rectangles now sample what
    the PlayStation draws (issue #179);
  - the village's deaths page draws its cause column at the Western discs'
    x under a Latin language (issue #177's follow-up).
- **Cheats** (off by default, on the Cheats sub-tab's two pages):
  - **No-fail minigames:** one switch under which a minigame cannot be lost.
    It covers the rope pull, the lighthouse valve, the wood chop, the well
    and the shisu (the shisu's score is raised to the best prize). The other
    minigames are unchanged until a recording reaches them;
  - **Enemy information:** every enemy's HP gauge and Identify stats from
    the first meeting, bosses too.
- **Battle:**
  - the name of an enemy's skill the party can still learn shows in blue
    (off by default; `unlearned_blue=1` in `bof3x.ini`);
  - the battle banner holds half again the characters under a Latin
    translation (issue #127), and fight 26's turn count uses the disc's
    digits (issue #133);
  - Skill Notes count the total (issue #118);
  - Weretiger's cross-fade draws (issue #189), and Station Myria's hologram
    draws without its box or the stripe at its edge (issue #191);
  - the dragon store prompt's hand, a battle's start clearing the target
    pick, and the rest of a set of eight small differences from the
    PlayStation are repaired.
- **Field and drawing:**
  - the world map's dial is drawn semi-transparent, its glass over the map as
    on the PlayStation;
  - Ryu no longer turns the wrong way in the Dauna Mine exchange, and the
    dragon-zombie puddle and circles are drawn whole (issue #142);
  - Garr's spear is no longer drawn over Ryu at the camp (issue #132), and
    Garr is no longer drawn over the block he stands behind in Angel Tower
    (issue #141);
  - the faerie board's Yes / No tile follows the line (issue #152);
  - Amalgam's melt draws with the right texels opaque (issue #111);
  - semi-transparent effects blend only the texels meant to blend;
  - the console flash in the lift rises instead of folding over, and the
    lift no longer stalls under instant text (issue #121);
  - a box or cell partly off the left or top edge clips instead of
    wrapping;
  - area 196's sand hill draws on a later showing in one session;
  - Sunder's animation at Mt. Myrneg's entry loops until the scene reaches
    him, a cut Capcom left unused (on by default; `BOF3X_ORIGINAL=SunderLoop`
    for the single pass).
- **The ending** (played and approved at 21:9): the *Fin* disc's darkness
  reaches the wide picture's corners; the bands stay black until the desert
  fades in; Rei and Garr scatter into pixels and Garr gathers back in
  Myria's chamber, as Nina and Momo did and as the PlayStation does; the
  disc holds at 1,000 and the night stays under the *Fin* panel, the camp
  scenes' look (`off=FinNight` for Capcom's lit desert). Area 198, Myria's
  chamber, is no longer pillarboxed in the wide modes.
- **Text:** **Med.** text speed is what **Fast** was, and **Fast** prints a
  page at once; a page still closes no sooner than Fast would have printed
  it, so a timed box can be read. An empty save slot draws no text on every
  install (`off=SaveSlotBlank` for the port's string).
- **Disc-built installs:**
  - **the loss tune plays** after a lost fight on a PlayStation-only or
    PSP-only install; it is built from your disc's battle music (alpha.6
    listed this as missing);
  - a European disc-only install takes its disc's own eight area sound
    banks, and a Western PlayStation disc-built image takes its disc's own
    layout for area 135's panel and the dock's boxes;
  - a Japanese disc-only install, and the PC under `ja-JP`, draw Japanese
    where they drew the port's Chinese or nothing; the Config screen's
    labels are left-aligned under a Japanese layer;
  - every byte of the image the disc does not hold has a recorded answer,
    and the game refuses an image with an unanswered one or one built by an
    older release (`BOF3X_EXEIMAGE_STRICT=0` runs it anyway);
  - Bone Dance's beat lengths (issue #149); area 108's cart put back when
    the area loads; Super Combo's prompts; exits that were missing or wrong
    (issues #130, #134, #139).
- **Extras** (off by default, on the Mods tab's Extras): a second faerie
  village entrance on the desert map, near Caer Xhan, with its ring of
  flowers drawn from your own disc's art.
- **The launcher's text** is in plain, short sentences throughout, and the
  Renderer box is gone (issue #124). The upgrade is faster: rows and layers
  that did not change are reused.
- **A 64-bit build** ships beside the 32-bit one as `bof3x-v0.1.0-beta.1-win-x64.zip`.
  It is a **developer build**: the same files, the same game folder, built
  for x86-64 Windows. The 32-bit zip is the default. Take the 64-bit zip only
  if you want to develop against it.
- **Under the hood:** the game's image is movable - our executable can be
  loaded at another address and plays the same - and the last page of
  Capcom's code our build kept is gone. Nothing a player sees changed; every
  scripted route hashes the same as before, at the linked address and at
  a moved one.

## ffmpeg (optional, recommended)

The opening logo movie from a disc, the PSP's sound effects and voices, the
PlayStation disc's recorded pieces (the rest jingles, the staff roll's
song), and the repair of the PC port's logo movie (Indeo 5, which current
Windows cannot play) are converted by **your** ffmpeg. Get a Windows build
from <https://ffmpeg.org/download.html#build-windows>, then put it on your
`PATH` or locate `ffmpeg.exe` in the launcher, and run the setup again.
Without it those pieces are skipped and the setup says which; the inn still
works, in silence.

## What works

- Installs from the PC port, from any one catalogued PlayStation disc alone,
  from a PSP disc alone, or a mix; English, French, German and Japanese from
  the matching disc, and any two of them together as a row above the text.
- A full play to the ending and the clear save, by hand: on a PC install
  with the furigana row above the text, and on a PlayStation US disc with
  a PSP European disc, in English. A French disc-only install is being
  played through.
- The PlayStation's sequenced music from your disc, the PSP's, or the PC's
  MP3s; the Jukebox in the launcher.
- Quicksaves (F6 / F7 / F5) alongside the game's own saves, in the field and
  in battle.
- Presets, and single changes turned off by name.
- Direct3D 11, windowed or borderless fullscreen, integer scaling, widescreen
  (16:9 and 21:9), the point filter and CRT looks, modern pads through SDL3,
  sound through SDL3.
- Signed executables (Authenticode, timestamped).
- The fixes and options listed in the project's README, each recorded in
  `docs/DIVERGENCE.md`.

## Known issues

- **Issue #190, a crash at the dragon gene window in battle** (after Data /
  Best and backing out of the slide-out). Reported by one player; the owner
  tried the reporter's steps at several places, in long and short plays, in
  bosses and in ordinary fights, and could not reproduce it. It stays open
  to gather data. **If it happens to you,** add to issue #190: the `CRASH n:`
  lines from `bof3x.log` in your folder, what the Sources page lists, and
  your language. **Do not attach the `bof3x.crash-*.dmp` files**: they hold
  the game's data.
- **Furigana does not build from this release** (SudachiPy is not bundled),
  and its readings are not proofread. A second language above the text
  works.
- With a row above the text, a one-line page puts the next-page arrow
  against the text's lower edge, and a box that waits on a timer's stop
  shows no arrow.
- At 21:9 a menu box that slides in can be seen whole in the side band on
  its first frames.
- Some labels and text painted into artwork stay Chinese on a PC-based
  install.
- **The launcher window** has been seen at 100 % display scaling only; at
  125 % or 150 % its picture is smoothed rather than sharp.
- **Not read yet:** `.chd` and `.cso` images (convert them back to
  `.cue`/`.bin` or `.iso`), and 2048-byte `.iso` images of a PlayStation disc,
  which have lost the disc's audio and movie sectors - use the `.bin`/`.cue`.
- The first setup takes several minutes and about 1 GB of disk for a disc
  install (the cache and the game folder); each pair of languages adds a
  few seconds more.
