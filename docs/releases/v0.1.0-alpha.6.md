**Breath of Fire III - bof3x v0.1.0-alpha.6** - a player release, an alpha.

bof3x is a renovation of the 2001 Chinese PC port of *Breath of Fire III*:
every function of the game's own code is our C++, our `bof3x.exe` runs it
with no code of Capcom's mapped, and the game can be installed from the PC
port, from a PlayStation disc alone, or from a PSP disc alone. **This
download holds no game data.** You add your own copy, and everything the game
reads is built on your machine from it.

## Getting started

1. Unzip into a folder with a **short path**, e.g. `C:\Games\bof3x`. Windows
   cannot play the opening movie from a path of about 160 characters or more.
2. Run `bof3x-launcher.exe`. The first time, it opens on the **Sources**
   page; later, it is under **Configure**, the **Data** tab.
3. On the **Sources** page, add what you own:
   - the PC port's game folder (the one holding `BOF3.exe` and `DAT`), the
     2001-04-18 Chinese release, unmodified;
   - a PlayStation disc as `.cue`/`.bin` - US, Japanese, European (English),
     French or German;
   - a PSP disc as `.iso` - Japanese or European;
   - or a `.zip` holding any of these;
   - or a whole folder of them: **Folder...** checks every image in it (and
     the folders below it) and adds each one that matches a known release.

   Each one is checked against the known releases by its files' hashes, and
   told apart in one line if it is not one. The first source makes the
   install; each later one adds what it carries (its language, the PSP's
   extras), and the install is rebuilt. Expect a few minutes per setup.
4. Play.

Everything is written inside the folder you unzipped: `sources.toml`,
`mods.toml`, `cache\`, `mods\` and `game\`. Your own files are only read.

**Upgrading from an earlier alpha:** unzip over the old folder and start the
launcher. It checks your install against the release and, when a piece of it
would be built differently, names the pieces and offers to rebuild them
(**No** starts nothing and leaves the install as it is). **A disc-built
install wants that rebuild this time:** several of the fixes below are in the
importer, which builds the game's code and exits from your disc. The rebuild
reuses what it can and is faster than in alpha.5. Your saves are kept.

## New since v0.1.0-alpha.5

- **Presets.** A new **Preset** page under **Settings** chooses how much of
  the project's repair you play with: **Recommended** (the default, what you
  had before), **As shipped** (the PC port's own behaviour where our code can
  still give it), **Repaired** (only the fixes to things that were broken) and
  **PlayStation** (what the PlayStation did where the PC port differed). The
  page lists what the chosen preset turns off for your language and install.
  A preset never turns a fix on; it only turns changes off. For a finer
  hand, `off=` in `bof3x.ini` names single changes by the name the project's
  ledger gives them.
- **Crashes fixed:**
  - the Gaist's turn-to-ash effect, which overran a buffer and crashed the
    fight (issue #163);
  - Giant Growth cast first in a session, at the hit pop-up;
  - Paralyzer, cast by an enemy with no sound cue table;
  - area 135, loading a save made in some of its zones.
- **Sound and music, as the PlayStation plays them:**
  - a sound effect that belongs to a sprite pans with its place on the
    screen, in the field and in battle;
  - the inn's night stops the town's song where it is and brings it back
    where it stopped; the PC port ran it into the jingle and lost it;
  - where the PlayStation stops a song it now cuts, where the PC port faded
    it over ten frames (the end of a fight, the title, chapter changes). `BOF3X_ORIGINAL=StopCuts` gives the fade back;
  - the area's song comes back after a jingle (issue #120);
  - a battle action's effect stops the sound-effect loops the last one left
    sounding;
  - the field's level cues play at their level, and the distance-gated ones
    only near their object.
- **Battle:**
  - the name of an enemy's skill the party can still learn shows in blue
    (off by default; `unlearned_blue=1` in `bof3x.ini`);
  - a Cheats-page switch shows **every enemy's HP gauge and Identify stats
    from the first meeting**, bosses too (off by default);
  - the battle banner holds half again the characters under a Latin
    translation (issue #127), and fight 26's turn count uses the disc's
    digits (issue #133);
  - Skill Notes count the total (issue #118);
  - the dragon store prompt's hand, a battle's start clearing the target
    pick, and the rest of a set of eight small differences from the
    PlayStation are repaired.
- **Field and drawing:**
  - the world map's dial is drawn semi-transparent, its glass over the map as
    on the PlayStation;
  - Ryu no longer turns the wrong way in the Dauna Mine exchange, and the
    dragon-zombie puddle and circles are drawn whole (issue #142);
  - Garr's spear is no longer drawn over Ryu at the camp (issue #132);
  - the faerie board's Yes / No tile follows the line (issue #152);
  - Amalgam's melt draws with the right texels opaque (issue #111);
  - semi-transparent effects blend only the texels meant to blend;
  - the console flash in the lift rises instead of folding over, and the
    lift no longer stalls under instant text (issue #121);
  - a box or cell partly off the left or top edge clips instead of
    wrapping;
  - Sunder's animation at Mt. Myrneg's entry loops until the scene reaches
    him, a cut Capcom left unused (on by default; `BOF3X_ORIGINAL=SunderLoop`
    for the single pass).
- **Text:** **Med.** text speed is what **Fast** was, and **Fast** prints a
  page at once; a page still closes no sooner than Fast would have printed
  it, so a timed box can be read.
- **Disc-built installs:** Bone Dance's beat lengths (issue #149); area 108's
  cart put back when the area loads; Super Combo's prompts; exits that were
  missing or wrong (issues #130, #134, #139). The loss tune is still missing
  (below).
- **The launcher's text** is in plain, short sentences throughout, and the
  Renderer box is gone (issue #124). The upgrade is faster: rows and layers
  that did not change are reused.
- **Under the hood:** the game's image is movable - our executable can be
  loaded at another address and plays the same - and the last page of
  Capcom's code our build kept is gone. Nothing a player sees changed; every
  scripted route hashes the same as before, at the linked address and at
  a moved one.

## ffmpeg (optional, recommended)

The opening logo movie from a disc, the PSP's sound effects and voices, the
PlayStation disc's recorded pieces (the rest jingles, the staff roll's
song), and the repair of the PC port's logo movie (Indeo 5, which current
Windows cannot play) are converted by **your** ffmpeg. Get a Windows build
from <https://ffmpeg.org/download.html#build-windows>, then put it on your
`PATH` or locate `ffmpeg.exe` in the launcher, and run the setup again.
Without it those pieces are skipped and the setup says which; the inn still
works, in silence.

## What works

- Installs from the PC port, from any one catalogued PlayStation disc alone,
  from a PSP disc alone, or a mix; English, French, German and Japanese from
  the matching disc, and any two of them together as a row above the text.
- The PlayStation's sequenced music from your disc, the PSP's, or the PC's
  MP3s; the Jukebox in the launcher.
- Quicksaves (F6 / F7 / F5) alongside the game's own saves.
- Presets, and single changes turned off by name.
- Direct3D 11, windowed or borderless fullscreen, integer scaling, widescreen
  (16:9 and 21:9), the point filter and CRT looks, modern pads through SDL3.
- Signed executables (Authenticode, timestamped).
- The fixes and options listed in the project's README, each recorded in
  `docs/DIVERGENCE.md`.

## Watched, not yet played through

This is an alpha. Much has been run by scripts and watched rather than played
by hand:

- **The sound changes** above are proven by measurement against the
  PlayStation's own code; nobody has listened to them through a play yet.
- **The Preset page and the enemy-information switch** are checked headless,
  not yet played.
- **The crash and drawing fixes** are each reproduced and re-run in the
  harness on the route that showed them; the game's bosses and every
  ability, enemy skill and item have been fought and cast by script, not by
  hand.
- **The row above the text, quicksaves, the button changes and the widescreen
  changes** stand as in alpha.5: checked by tests and captures, not yet
  played.
- **The disc-only and PSP-only installs** have passed scripted runs and the
  attract sequence, not a playthrough; a PlayStation-only install has been
  played by players and reported on.
- French, German and Japanese are less tested than English.
- **The launcher window** has been seen at 100 % display scaling only; at
  125 % or 150 % its picture is smoothed rather than sharp.

## Known issues

- **Furigana does not build from this release** (SudachiPy is not bundled),
  and its readings are not proofread. A second language above the text
  works.
- With a row above the text, a one-line page puts the next-page arrow
  against the text's lower edge, and a box that waits on a timer's stop
  shows no arrow.
- On a PlayStation-only or PSP-only install, losing a fight replays the
  fight's own song instead of the loss tune: the loss tune is not in those
  installs.
- Some labels and text painted into artwork stay Chinese on a PC-based
  install.
- **Not read yet:** `.chd` and `.cso` images (convert them back to
  `.cue`/`.bin` or `.iso`), and 2048-byte `.iso` images of a PlayStation disc,
  which have lost the disc's audio and movie sectors - use the `.bin`/`.cue`.
- The first setup takes several minutes and about 1 GB of disk for a disc
  install (the cache and the game folder); each pair of languages adds a
  few seconds more.
